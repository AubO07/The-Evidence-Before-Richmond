Six Findings for Richmond Review

01 / KNOWLEDGE & ASSESSMENT

A caring role denied

Ground 1 of my formal complaint. Failure to recognise and properly assess my continuing caring role, despite evidence of ongoing care and professional observations following Dee Gallagher’s letter.

The allegation

Recognition of an ongoing caring role was withdrawn or denied despite evidence of continuing practical care.

What Richmond knew

The 18-month joint tenancy, care history, Conor’s repeated home use, and subsequent MDT visits were relevant to the assessment.

The documentary contradiction

Dee Gallagher’s 24 July 2025 assertion should be tested against the later home observations and carer-assessment correspondence. The DWP cessation date reportedly predates Dee’s letter.

What Richmond must explain

Who determined that regular and substantial care had ended; what assessment and consultation supported that decision; what effect it had on involvement, support and benefits.

What remedy is required?

Identify the decision records, correct any inaccurate carer-status account, review exclusion from care discussions and withdrawn support, and refer benefit entitlement questions to the responsible decision-maker.

02 / ACCOUNTABILITY FINDING

A promised resolution replaced by inaccurate accounts

Ground 2 of my formal complaint. Failure to complete the promised financial reconciliation or explain what happened to Conor’s rent contributions, together with the communication of an account of his residence that conflicts with evidence of his continuing use of our home.

Documented sequence

March–April 2026. Aubrey supplied the tenancy, rent ledger, bank evidence and reconciliation, identifying £13,156 in payments made towards Conor's share in addition to Aubrey's own contributions. He requested a calm, supported family discussion with Conor to establish the figures, understand his obligations, investigate any redirected funds and seek an affordable resolution.

22 April 2026. Dee Gallagher confirmed in writing that Denisha Bigik-Ojede would address the financial matter with Conor and report back. No completed reconciliation or substantive outcome was communicated to Aubrey in the records reviewed.

5 August 2026. Denisha wrote to the landlord and his legal representatives: Connor moved from the property in March 2026 and is no longer residing there. Her correspondence did not address the outstanding rent reconciliation.

Contrary evidence. Timestamped photographs and witness statements document Conor's returns, overnight stays, meals, clothing, belongings, laundry and ordinary domestic activity at Cambridge Park after March 2026. The evidence challenges the categorical departure account; supported accommodation and continuing use of a family home are not necessarily mutually exclusive.

Verification boundary: The 5 August communication is identified. A second allegedly inaccurate residence letter and proof of precisely which photographs Denisha personally received before that date still require verification. Neither is presented here as established.

Statutory responsibilities to examine

Care Act 2014, section 1: Richmond's wellbeing functions include considering relevant personal dignity, control over day-to-day life, family relationships, economic wellbeing and suitability of living accommodation when exercising functions under Part 1.

Care Act 2014, section 6: cooperation duties apply to relevant functions and partners; section 6(4) requires arrangements for cooperation between relevant adult care and housing officers. This does not itself create an unconditional duty to pay a private tenancy debt.

Care Act 2014, section 9(5): where a needs assessment was being carried out, involvement of Conor, any carer and others he wished to involve must be examined in light of the statutory requirements.

Questions requiring a response: Was a supported financial meeting arranged? Was Conor shown and helped to understand the reconciliation? Were possible redirected payments examined? What residence information was checked before it was communicated externally? Was any inaccurate record corrected?

These are legal duties and questions for examination, not a determination that any particular duty was breached. Section 1 · Section 6 · Section 9

What remained unresolved

The supplied financial figures did not produce a reported settlement or completed family discussion. The official residence account did not address Conor's continuing obligations under the joint tenancy, and was challenged by dated evidence of his home use.

Subsequent financial hardship and housing consequences are documented separately. Their causes and the responsibilities of different organisations must be assessed without treating sequence alone as proof of causation.

03 / WARNINGS & RESPONSE

The warnings went unanswered

Ground 3 of my formal complaint. Failure to respond adequately to documented warnings of financial harm and homelessness, including requests for assistance, reported failures of the housing application process, and the need to establish whether the statutory 56-day homelessness prevention duty was properly recognised and discharged.

56-day homelessness prevention duty — my housing application. Despite repeated warnings of foreseeable homelessness, the provision of tenancy and financial evidence, and documented attempts to access housing assistance, the Borough has not adequately explained when it recognised its statutory prevention duty, what reasonable steps it took, or why the information already available did not lead to effective preventive action before my eviction on 1 September 2026. The 56-day period is not a guarantee of housing within that time; the issue is whether the Council fulfilled the applicable assessment, planning and reasonable-steps duties. See the statutory responsibilities section ↗

The allegation

Repeated financial and housing warnings, including foreseeable risks to the family’s welfare and continuity of care, were not adequately resolved before the loss of the home.

What Richmond knew

The March 2026 rent reconciliation and financial warnings communicated to the MDT; correspondence with Housing; meetings with Don Masaki; reported portal failures; and approaching possession and eviction dates. Receipt, escalation and response should be established for each warning.

The documentary contradiction

The housing evidence register identifies 111 substantive messages, four evidenced appointments and a witnessed system problem on 27 May 2026. Citizens Advice assisted with application 290767 on 2 June. The reconciliation and witness statements document financial warnings; whether each warning prompted an adequate response remains to be tested.

What Richmond must explain

For each financial, housing or welfare warning: who received it, when, what risk was identified, whether it was escalated across MDT, Housing and Benefits, and what protective or preventive action followed? Disclose the original IT referral and outcome, application/error logs and any manual assistance offered. For my housing application, provide the application and assessment records, the date on which prevention duties were accepted or declined, my personalised housing plan and reviews, and a dated account of reasonable preventive steps taken in response to the warnings.

What remedy is required?

Produce a dated warning-and-response chronology; investigate failed housing submissions and IT referral; disclose relevant records; identify missed opportunities to prevent foreseeable harm; and provide reasoned decisions on housing duties and any remedies.

Additional source to integrate: Richmond Housing Evidence Addendum (25 September 2026), Don correspondence and housing application 290767. These records are not yet linked from the 331-page consolidated evidence bundle.

Financial evidence ignored

Ground 4 of my formal complaint. Failure to provide a substantive response to the tenancy, rent ledger, bank payments and reconciliation supplied, including the documented explanation of how the arrears arose.

The allegation

The completed rent and bank reconciliation was supplied but no adequate engagement with its central figures has been demonstrated.

What Richmond knew

The signed tenancy, landlord’s ledger, bank payments, Conor’s contributions, benefit records and a completed reconciliation—not merely a request to investigate.

The documentary contradiction

The accounting identifies 29 of 36 half-rent obligations paid by Aubrey, including £13,156 towards Conor’s share. After a £6,817 payment in October 2025, the ledger reportedly showed £0.85 outstanding at that point.

What Richmond must explain

Which entries or assumptions it disputes, why the supplied reconciliation was not substantively addressed, and how subsequent arrears and benefit decisions were assessed. The later judgment is not erased by the October 2025 balance.

What remedy is required?

Provide an itemised response to the completed reconciliation and bank evidence, correct any demonstrable accounting errors, and identify any appropriate benefit, housing or legal redress without treating the court judgment as automatically overturned.

05 / CONOR'S TESTIMONY & AGENCY

Conor's voice unheard

Ground 5 of my formal complaint. Failure to give proper consideration to Conor’s own testimony and expressed wishes, including his account of the landlord’s representations and their influence on his departure.

The allegation

Conor's direct testimony, written instructions and expressed wishes were not demonstrably reconciled with the landlord's account and professional records subsequently relied upon in decisions affecting him. This is an allegation requiring a documented response, not a finding that every recipient deliberately disregarded him.

What the recipients knew

Conor explained to Mary that the landlord's statements about rising rent influenced his departure; he endorsed a joint account on 1 March 2026 and handed over a signed statement at the recorded March 2026 MDT meeting. His later written authority sought independent consideration of his account and expressly permitted his father to present evidence. Recipients and their responsibilities must be identified individually.

The documentary contradiction

Conor's explanation of why he left should be tested against the executed 18-month tenancy and the reported representations of a 12-month term, a rent increase and leaving without penalty. His continued contact with his father and requests for his involvement challenge any unqualified account that he had severed that relationship or wished his father excluded. These records do not by themselves establish the motives of others or every causal link.

What must be explained

Who reviewed Conor's recordings, statement, emails and instructions; what contemporaneous record shows that consideration; what was checked against the tenancy and other primary evidence; and how his wishes influenced decisions? Separately, how did the court treat his written statement and audio evidence in light of Denisha's letter, and what did the court actually find about whether Conor had testified? The court's reasoning must be checked against the judgment or transcript; Richmond cannot answer for the court.

What remedy is required?

Provide an indexed record of receipt, review, referral and response; correct demonstrably inaccurate accounts; give Conor an opportunity to speak independently and with his chosen support; review the effect of any failure on his care, accommodation, finances and autonomy; and identify the appropriate separate route for challenging the court's treatment of his evidence.

Conor's present understanding: His father reports that Conor now regards the landlord's conduct as coercive. Conor should confirm the precise wording of that present view before it is attributed to him as a direct quotation. The claimed links to distress, detention, injury, judgment, property loss and restricted independence warrant investigation, not automatic conclusions of causation.

06 / CONSEQUENCES

The cost of what followed

Ground 6 of my formal complaint. Failure to address the continuing consequences of these matters, including financial hardship, housing insecurity, disruption to care and the unresolved handling of our possessions. My immediate priority is financial assistance and resolution of the losses I believe the Council’s actions or omissions caused or contributed to.

The allegation

Decisions and omissions across care, benefits and housing produced a cumulative sequence of serious consequences requiring a corporate response.

What Richmond knew

The risks to carer support, family involvement, rent, housing security and Conor’s continuity of care were communicated over time.

The documentary contradiction

Contemporary correspondence and financial records record interrupted support, accommodation changes, court proceedings, eviction, homelessness and loss of belongings. Their chronology is established more clearly than every causal link.

What Richmond must explain

Which department made each decision, its evidential and statutory basis, whether cross-service safeguarding and cooperation occurred, and what remedy is proposed for substantiated failures? For missing property, who authorised removal, storage or disposal, when, and on what record?

What remedy is required?

Provide a coordinated corporate response, address continuing housing safety, investigate the whereabouts and handling of missing belongings, assess their recoverability and material value alongside welfare impacts, and identify appropriate complaints, review or compensation routes. Examine whether any failures to assess or take reasonable steps under the homelessness prevention duty (Finding 03) materially contributed to my eviction, homelessness and subsequent losses, distinguishing established events from causal matters requiring investigation.

01

Financial consequences

The Carer’s Allowance and DWP status history, interrupted benefits or housing support, payments made to sustain the tenancy, depleted resources and subsequent liabilities. Loss of possessions is also a potential material financial loss: the Council should establish an inventory, disposal or storage authority, recoverability, valuation and any appropriate remedy. The March 2025 DWP record requires separate explanation from the July MDT letter.

02

Legal consequences

The signed 18-month tenancy, reported landlord representations, unresolved rent warnings, possession proceedings, September 2026 judgment, eviction and continuing legal challenges.

03

Well-being

Reduced communication and involvement in Conor’s care, repeated accommodation changes, homelessness, loss of possessions and their personal significance, disrupted family stability and continuing concerns about recovery and safety. The missing belongings also require a financial assessment, addressed separately above.

THE EVIDENCE NOW BEFORE RICHMOND · HOUSING REF. 57580

PURPOSE OF THIS SUBMISSION

This submission presents six interconnected findings, supported by contemporaneous, cross-referenced evidence. Its purpose is to assist an investigation already underway, ensure that Conor’s evidence is heard, and secure action to prevent further harm from unresolved decisions and disputed accounts.

01

SUPPORT THE BOROUGH’S ONGOING INVESTIGATION

Assist the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames in its ongoing homelessness assessment and related enquiries, as confirmed by Ms Iyabo Soniran, Deputy Homelessness Prevention and Solutions Manager, on 19 November 2026.

Provide direct access to the original evidence supporting the six findings: correspondence, the signed tenancy, financial records, dated photographs, professional communications, witness statements and recordings.

This includes material challenging the landlord’s allegations of rent arrears and antisocial behaviour.

Ask Richmond to confirm that it will examine the evidence, identify further enquiries, and explain how its findings will inform decisions and appropriate remedies.

02

ENSURE CONOR’S OWN VOICE IS HEARD

Conor’s circumstances have repeatedly been described through other people’s accounts, despite his own contemporaneous testimony, signed statements, voice recordings and expressed wishes.

His evidence addresses representations made about the tenancy, the circumstances influencing his departure, his continuing family relationship and his wishes concerning his father’s involvement.

Conor has authorised the presentation of his evidence and is willing to provide his testimony directly in a safe and appropriately supported setting.

Ask Richmond to consider that evidence independently, offer Conor an opportunity to speak directly, and explain how his testimony and wishes inform its assessment.

Read Finding 05 — Conor’s testimony and agency ↗

03

EXAMINE FAILURES AND ADDRESS THEIR CONTINUING CONSEQUENCES

The six findings raise questions about verification of Conor’s joint tenancy, the professional accounts of his residence, recognition of his father’s caring role, unresolved financial reconciliation and repeated warnings of foreseeable housing, legal and financial harm.

Ask Richmond to establish what each service knew, what original evidence it checked, whether errors or omissions occurred, and what corrective or protective action is required.

This includes action concerning continuing housing insecurity, financial hardship, missing possessions, family relationships, dignity and independence.

OUTCOME SOUGHT

A coordinated, reasoned response to the six findings, identifying what has been examined, what is established or disputed, who is responsible for further action, and what remedies will be considered.

The purpose is to assist the ongoing investigation, ensure that Conor’s evidence is heard, and secure meaningful action to prevent further harm.
